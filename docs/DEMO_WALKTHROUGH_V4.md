# FieldMark — demo walkthrough, v4

6 August 2026. Replaces v3, which predates the evidence layer, the
rising rebuild, and the design pass.

Running time: ~17 minutes at a comfortable pace. Cut points marked.

---

## What changed from v3, and why the structure moved

**v3 interleaved the three cohorts through the demo and put Community
at beat 14.** That was right when Community was a practice-pattern
story. It isn't any more — Community is now the surface with the
strongest evidence, and it should land before the close, not after
everything else.

**The argument has a second half now.** v3's spine was *system of
memory versus system of retrieval*. That still holds and it's still
the close. But the platform can now do something it couldn't in
August: **prove, from federal data, that a specific physician treats
lung cancer.** That's a claim no competitor makes, and it earns its
own act.

**Two discovery stories exist that didn't before.** Melina Marmarelis
was invisible to the platform 48 hours ago and is now ranked first in
the US and first globally among rising stars. James Uyeki has no
publications and a defensible NSCLC evidence record. Both are better
than the abstract version of the same claim.

**The whole platform was redesigned.** One typographic register, one
palette, one hero pattern, consistent chrome. You no longer need to
avoid surfaces because they look different.

Structure is now three acts: **what the platform knows before you meet
anyone**, **what happens once you engage**, and **the close**.

## Before you record — six checks

1. **Walk the whole path once, signed in.** Several surfaces were
   rebuilt in the last 72 hours.
2. **Verify every number you say aloud.** Figures below are current as
   of 6 August but the cohorts rescore. Anything marked ⚠️ moves.
3. **Reset your follow-up dates the morning you record.** They're
   relative-offset now; run the SQL and they'll re-anchor.
4. **Warm the brief** for whichever HCP you use in Beat 9. Briefs

*Nav: PEOPLE → Community cohort toggle.*

**This is the strongest evidence beat and the clearest competitive
claim. It was beat 14 in the last script. It belongs here.**

> Last cohort, and the one I'd argue nobody else covers.
>
> Every KOL database has the top fifty names in NSCLC. What nobody has
> is the community oncologist in your territory who's treating lung
> cancer patients this week and has never published a paper.
>
> The problem is that community physicians leave almost no scientific
> footprint. So we had to find a different kind of evidence.

*Point at the ledger header and the filter chips.*

> This is thirteen thousand community oncologists, and the default
> view is eleven hundred of them — because those are the ones where we
> have actual evidence of NSCLC practice.

*Point at the chips: ANCHORED · SUPPORTED · CANDIDATES · NO MEDICARE
EVIDENCE.*

> **Anchored** means we can prove it. **Supported** means the evidence
> is consistent but not exclusive. **Candidates** are plausible with no
> disease-specific evidence. And where there's no Medicare evidence at
> all, we say that rather than hiding them.

*Point at a row's evidence chip.*

> The proof is on the row. This physician prescribed osimertinib in
> 2022, 2023 and 2024.

*[Pause]*

> Osimertinib treats EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer. That is
> its only indication. There is no second reading of that
> prescription.

*Open Uyeki, or Hussein if he's your lead profile. ⚠️ Confirm rank
before you name it.*

> James Uyeki, community oncologist in Austin. Zero publications.
> Nothing a traditional KOL database would surface.
>
> Here's what we know about him.

*Point at the evidence line under his name.*

> Prescribed osimertinib in 2022, 2023 and 2024. Three consecutive
> years — a materially stronger claim than one.

*Scroll to Medicare Administered Therapy.*

> And this is what he administers in his own office. Nine agents,
> twenty-five reported cells, three years. Pembrolizumab to
> sixty-three Medicare patients in 2023, across two hundred and
> eighty-six administration days.

*Point at the pemetrexed row.*

> Pemetrexed carries a badge — it's the one agent here that's
> genuinely lung-anchored. The others are given across most solid
> tumours, so we don't claim them as lung evidence. The badge
> describes the drug's labelled use, not this physician's case mix.

*Point at an under-floor cell.*

> And where a year is missing, it says why. Medicare suppresses any
> provider-drug-year cell under eleven beneficiaries — so this reads
> "under floor, fewer than eleven," not blank and not zero. Absence

*This is the close of the beat. Say it plainly.*

> Two independent federal sources. Part D tells us what he prescribes.
> Part B tells us what he infuses. Neither one was built for this
> purpose and both are public.
>
> That's a community oncologist we can put in front of an MSL with
> evidence, and I don't know of another platform that can.

## Questions you should expect

**"Where does the data come from?"**
PubMed and OpenAlex for publications and co-authorship.
ClinicalTrials.gov for trials. NPPES for identity and practice
location. CMS Open Payments, Medicare Part B and Medicare Part D for
engagement, administration and prescribing. Public registries, all of
them.

**"How do you know the physician is who you say?"**
We match by name against the federal registry and we're explicit that
it's a match. Below a confidence threshold we hold it out of the
surface rather than showing it.

**"How can you say someone treats lung cancer from a prescription?"**
Because some drugs have exactly one indication. Osimertinib treats
EGFR-mutant NSCLC and nothing else. We restrict the anchor to agents
with no material second indication and grade the rest separately. Any
drug that's given across tumour types is shown but never used as
proof.

**"Why is the community board eleven hundred and not thirteen
thousand?"**
Because eleven hundred is what we have evidence for. The rest are
visible one click away and labelled as candidates. We'd rather show a
smaller defensible set than a larger plausible one.

**"What about physician privacy?"**
Everything shown is public professional record — published work,
registered trials, federal payment and claims disclosures. There's a
report-data-issue path and an opt-out request on every profile.
*(The opt-out mechanism is specced, not enforced. Don't overclaim.)*

**"Can it do other therapeutic areas?"**
NSCLC is fully built, atopic dermatitis is second and partially built.
The architecture is TA-scoped throughout — the constraint is data
ingestion time, not engineering. One honest caveat: the Medicare
evidence layer works best in adult oncology, where the drugs are
indication-specific and the patients are Medicare-age. It degrades
outside that.

**"How current is it?"**
Publications weekly. Trial status weekly. Trial discovery and Medicare
are less frequent, and every surface states its own as-of date.
Nothing claims to be live that isn't.
