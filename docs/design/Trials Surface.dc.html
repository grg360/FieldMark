Trials, as its own surface

A destination at /trials. The asset-page section stays and keeps answering the drug-scoped question in place. This surface takes the questions that cut across drugs — territory, roster, sponsor, setting — which a section inside one asset structurally cannot reach. Same vocabulary, same disclosure lines, same handling of role and matching. Larger sibling, not a second voice.

01  SPINE
Trials are the rows. Everything else is a lens.
The same records support four surfaces. Investigators and sponsors both make lists where a row means something different each time — a person, an organisation. Trials are the only unit where every row is the same kind of fact and every other axis survives as a filter on it. An MSL planning a week is choosing between trials, not between sponsors.
02  TERRITORY
Scope above the list, not a dropdown inside it.
Territory is the reason this exists, so it sits in a band of its own between the header and everything else, and every count below re-states itself under it. A dropdown would make the national set the real view and territory an adjustment to it. It is the other way round.
03  COVERAGE
The scope band says what it cannot see.
Sixty-six open trials have no resolved institution for any matched investigator. They can never appear in a territory view. That sentence lives on the band itself, not in the footnotes, because it is the one caveat that changes what the list in front of you means.
04  ROSTER
A lens, and a standing strip. Not the default.
Nine of thirty-three tracked HCPs are on an open trial — high value, but too small to be the whole surface, and a default that hides 271 trials teaches the wrong thing about what we hold. So: a named strip under the scope band that answers it at a glance, and one click makes it the filter.
05  SPONSOR
Named per trial, countable as a filter, never a landscape.
Sponsor is a public field on the record; withholding it would be the editorial act. It is named in every row and it filters. What we do not build is a sponsor page — a view whose whole subject is a competitor turns a registry field into a scoreboard, and a count of open trials is not a claim about a company.
06  ROLE
Investigator. Never lead, never site.
ClinicalTrials.gov labels every site lead Principal Investigator — one record here carries sixty-three. A person named on a trial may run it or may run one site of it, and we cannot tell which. The word is investigator everywhere, including in the territory band, which is scoped by institution and not by who leads.
07  NO CHANGE FEED
Two dates instead of an activity stream.
The table is overwritten in place, so there is no new, no newly recruiting, no sort by recency of change. The header carries the crawl date, which is when the set was last completed, and the refresh date, which is when the statuses were last true. Those are the only temporal claims available.
08  EVERY ROW LEAVES
Registry, profile, asset — three exits per row.
The NCT id opens the record on ClinicalTrials.gov, which is the source of truth. Investigator names open their profiles. Interventions that are one of the thirty-eight roster assets open the asset page; the rest are plain text, because a link to nothing is a promise we did not keep.
