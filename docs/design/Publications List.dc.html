WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY
1
HIERARCHY
Citation count leads as a figure in its own column — it is the one number that separates a landmark from a routine paper, and a column lets the eye scan it without reading. Title follows, in serif, at the only size that varies. Journal and PMID recede to a single provenance line at 10px. The card is gone; rows share one grid so eleven fields stop competing.
2
ROLE BECOMES STRUCTURE
Role stopped being a chip. It is the default grouping, a gold rule, a larger title, and a gold citation figure. Six senior-author papers sit above thirty-eight co-authored ones, so the answer to “what is his” is the first thing on the page. In DETAIL rows his surname is picked out of the author string in gold — position shown as a fact, never computed into a number the truncated data cannot support.
3
DENSITY
LEDGER rows carry title, source and citations only — roughly twenty-six to a screen against twelve today. DETAIL adds authors and the absence phrases. Nothing collapses by default and nothing is paginated: the count in every band header equals the rows beneath it, and the footer states shown against held.
4
SORTS, AND DISCUSSED
Three orders: CONTRIBUTION, MOST CITED, RECENT. Discussed was never an order — with threads on two of forty-four papers it sorted a rounding error to the top. It is now a filter that states its own count, and the row-level affordance appears only where a thread exists. Papers without one show nothing; there is no “ask the first question”.
5
SPARSE FIELDS
Assets, study type and theme share the provenance line and simply shorten when absent — no label, no reserved row, so three-quarters of rows read as complete rather than empty. Open access earns the right column, the one place where an absence is stated: “Subscription only — no full text held.”
