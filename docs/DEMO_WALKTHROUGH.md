# FieldMark — demo walkthrough script

Version 2, 4 August 2026. Replaces the walkthrough pulled after
the Hussein data-integrity issue.

Running time: roughly 12–14 minutes at a comfortable pace. Cut
Congress and Social first if you need to come in under 10.

---

## Before you start — three checks

1. **Sign in and walk the path once.** Several surfaces were
   rebuilt in the last 72 hours and some have only been verified
   from payloads, not rendered.
2. **Rehearse the Intelligence question.** The content check
   rejects recommendation phrasing, off-label vocabulary, and any
   real physician name from the database. Draft your question and
   test it before you are live.
3. **Have Heymach's profile and the Trials surface pre-loaded** in
   background tabs. Both take a moment on first paint.

---

## The through-line

Say this once, early, and let every surface prove it rather than
repeating it:

> Every number on this platform can be traced to a source, and
> where we don't know something, we say so rather than showing
> you a zero.

That is the differentiator. Not the data — competitors have
publication data. It is that the platform tells you what it
cannot see, which is what makes the parts it can see usable in
front of a physician.

## 1. Sign-in — 30 seconds

**Show:** the landing screen. Constellation, tagline.

**Say:**

> "We see the nebula, not just the star." The premise is that the
> people who matter in a therapeutic area next year are visible
> now, in the literature and in the claims data, if you're
> looking at the whole field rather than the twenty names
> everybody already knows.

> Access is invite-gated and verified — we check MSL role through
> LinkedIn, and an MSL's identity is never shared with the
> physicians they're researching.

**Don't linger.** It's a login screen.

## 2. Home — 90 seconds

**Show:** the masthead figures, WHAT MOVED, NEXT 3 ACTIONS.

**Say:**

> This is what an MSL sees on a Monday. Four figures at the top:
> overdue follow-ups, open follow-ups, portfolio size, and rising
> star coverage in their territory.

**Point at WHAT MOVED.**

> This is rank movement in the rising-star cohort, and note what
> it says — "compared against 8 June 2026." Not "this week."
> We hold one snapshot, so that's the only honest comparison, and
> the surface names the date rather than implying a weekly cadence
> it can't support.

**This is the first proof point. Let it land.**

**Point at NEXT 3 ACTIONS.**

> Ordered by priority label, then due date — and it says so.
> There's a "why now" line under each: *"Same institution as
> Lito — one visit can carry both conversations."* That's the
> product thesis in one sentence. It's not telling you who's
> important; it's telling you what to do with your Tuesday.

## 3. The Week — 60 seconds

**Show:** the fortnight view.

**Say:**

> This is what happened to the people you track. Publications,
> trials, congress, social — scoped to your portfolio, not the
> whole therapeutic area.

**Point at WHAT WAS CHECKED.**

> And this is the part I'd draw your attention to. Even in a
> quiet week, the surface tells you what it looked at:
> publications ingested through this date, trials refreshed on
> that date, congress between meetings, four of thirty-three
> social accounts verified.

> A user who opens this on a Tuesday and sees nothing has still
> learned something — that nothing was missed. Most competitor
> tools show you an empty feed and let you assume you're
> up to date.

**If the social line is visible, read it:**

> "Silence here is our coverage, not theirs." We can't say
> whether the other twenty-nine posted, because we don't have
> verified accounts for them. So we don't.

## 4. People — the cohort ledger — 2 minutes

**Show:** the Established ledger.

**Say:**

> Nearly three thousand established NSCLC investigators, ranked.
> Two signals produce the ranking: scientific influence, from
> publication leadership, and network influence, from
> co-authorship centrality.

**Point at the header.**

> "Pharma excluded." Industry engagement is displayed but not
> ranked on — only about a third of the cohort has Open Payments
> data attributable to NSCLC, so weighting it would measure
> whether we matched someone to a registry, not how engaged they
> are. So we show it and don't score it.

**Point at the band header — SPREAD, TREAT AS TIED.**

> And this is the honesty that I think is genuinely rare. These
> top rows are separated by fractions of a percentile. Rather
> than pretending rank one is meaningfully ahead of rank three,
> the surface says the spread is within the resolution of the
> measure and to treat them as tied.

**Open a row.**

> The drawer shows what placed the row there, with the trace
> underneath — percentile within cohort, methodology version, and
> a link out to each component.

*(Note: this drawer is being redesigned. If it still restates the
row, don't dwell — click through to a profile instead.)*

**Mention the cohorts:**

> Three cohorts — established, rising stars, and community. And
> the industry filter runs across all of them: we removed 271
> pharma and regulatory employees from the established board
> this weekend. An FDA reviewer isn't someone you engage, and a
> Merck medical director is a competitor's employee.

## 5. An HCP profile — 3 minutes

**Show:** Heymach, or your strongest example.

This is the longest stop. It's the surface an MSL actually opens
before a meeting.

**Start with the signal summary.**

> One paragraph. And what it does is lead with stance — what this
> investigator argues, where they've pushed back, what they treat
> as settled. Every claim in it traces to a sourced position
> below. It isn't a summary of their CV; it's a summary of their
> position.

**Scroll to the Belief Profile.**

> This is the heart of it. Positions extracted from their own
> published work, banded by how strongly they're asserted, each
> one with the papers behind it — journal, year, PMID, author
> role.

**Pick one and read a fragment.**

> This is what a competitor database can't give you. A KOL
> database tells you someone is influential in NSCLC. This tells
> you they argue unresectable stage III EGFR-mutant disease is a
> distinct context with its own evidentiary standard, not a
> downstream application of FLAURA logic. That's what you'd
> actually want to know before you walk in.

**Scroll to Field Insights.**

> And this is the crowdsourced layer. MSL-captured observations
> — a tumour board comment, a conference exchange — linked to the
> published position they corroborate or complicate. Structured
> input only, no free text, anonymous contributor with verified
> identity.

> That's the loop: published record on one side, field
> observation on the other, connected.

**Scroll to The Record and Administered Volume.**

> Publication record, collaborator network, and then this —
> Medicare Part B administered volume. What they actually infuse,
> at what scale.

> And look at how it's framed. "Beneficiary instances" — counted
> per beneficiary per code, a patient given three agents counts
> three times. It measures administration, not headcount. Part B
> fee-for-service only, so it's a floor and never a total.

> Every one of those caveats is on the surface, not in a footnote.

**If Contact & Access is populated, mention it:**

> Institutional contact, published sources only. If we can't find
> a published email, we say so rather than guessing at a format.

## 7. Drugs and Trials — 2 minutes

**Show:** the Drugs Index.

**Say:**

> Every asset in the therapeutic area, organised by molecular
> target — because that's how treatment is selected and how a
> territory is worked.

**Point at the publication counts.**

> These are publications mentioning the agent within the
> lung-cancer corpus. Not papers "about" it — mentioning it. The
> distinction matters and the surface makes it.

**Open an asset, scroll to Trials.**

> And this is where a drug connects to people. Every open lung
> trial naming an investigator we've matched to the ranked
> cohort. Phase, status, sponsor, and the named investigators on
> each.

**Then go to the Trials surface.**

> Same data, different question. Two hundred and two open trials
> across the roster. And the lenses are what make it useful —
> territory, your roster, sponsor, phase.

**Point at the roster strip.**

> Nine of the thirty-three people you track are on an open trial.
> Named, one click to filter.

**Point at the territory coverage line.**

> "One hundred and forty-eight of the two hundred and two open
> trials name an investigator whose state resolves. The other
> fifty-four appear in no territory view — the territory counts
> are a floor."

> Again: we tell you what we can't see.

**One caveat to state plainly, because a pharma audience will
ask:**

> We say investigator, never principal investigator.
> ClinicalTrials.gov labels every site lead that way — one record
> here carries sixty-three — so we can't tell who leads a study
> and we don't claim to.

## 8. Pulse — 90 seconds

**Show:** the themes board.

**Say:**

> Where scientific attention is moving in the therapeutic area.
> Twenty-five themes, ranked by share of publications in the
> window.

**Point at the two-window strip.**

> And this is the most careful thing on the platform. Two
> windows — the headline figures use one, movement uses another,
> because two months in our ingest history are contaminated. One
> was a backfill recovery, one was the tail of an ingest gap.
> Both would register as attention movement when they're
> artefacts.

> So movement compares April–May against February–March, and the
> page says so.

**Point at the movement caveat.**

> And then this: "movement figures remain unreliable until three
> clean ingest cycles have run. Do not carry a figure in this
> column into a conversation as an established trend."

> That sentence is on the product. We'd rather tell an MSL not to
> use a number than have them repeat it to a physician and be
> wrong.

## 9. Intelligence — the live moment — 2 minutes

This is the strongest thing in the demo because it's
participatory. Do it live.

**Show:** the forum index.

**Say:**

> Anchored discussion. Every thread is anchored to a published
> paper — a thread can't exist without one, and the anchor
> defines what's on topic. Topic discussion only: no HCP names in
> posts, no product claims, no discussion of unapproved use.

**Point at the disclosure banner.**

> And note the labelling. Content marked SEEDED is fabricated for
> compliance review. Content marked LIVE is authored on this
> surface by a verified MSL, and it's checked for recommendation
> language, off-label content and physician names before
> publishing.

**Now go to a bibliography, pick a paper, and create a thread.**

> So here's the workflow. I'm on a physician's publication list.
> I find a paper I genuinely want to discuss — I click through,
> and I ask a question anchored to it.

**Type your rehearsed question. Submit. Land on the thread.**

> And there it is, marked LIVE, sitting alongside the seeded
> content but clearly distinguished from it.

> That's the crowdsourced intelligence loop — it starts from the
> literature, not from a blank box. Which is what makes it
> compliant and what makes it useful.

## Closing — 60 seconds

**Say:**

> Three things I'd want you to take away.

> **One.** The rising-star and community cohorts are the point.
> Every KOL database has the top fifty names in NSCLC. What
> nobody has is the community oncologist in your territory who's
> administering pembrolizumab to eighty patients a year and has
> never published, or the early-career investigator whose
> trajectory says they'll matter in eighteen months.

> **Two.** The honesty is the product, not an apology for it. An
> MSL taking a number into a physician conversation needs to know
> which figures are load-bearing and which aren't. Every surface
> here tells you what it can't see. That's what makes the rest of
> it usable.

> **Three.** It refreshes. This isn't a static database you
> license once a year. Publications ingest weekly, trial status
> refreshes weekly, the cohorts rescore, and the movement
> accrues. It's a live intelligence subscription, not a
> directory.

## Questions you should expect

**"Where does the data come from?"**
PubMed and OpenAlex for publications and co-authorship.
ClinicalTrials.gov for trials. NPPES for identity and practice
location. CMS Open Payments and Medicare Part B for engagement
and administered volume. Public registries, all of them.

**"How do you know the physician is who you say?"**
We match by name against NPPES, and we're explicit that it's a
match. Where confidence is below threshold we hold it out of the
surface rather than showing it. On the social side we
hand-reviewed every one of a hundred and fifty-one attributions
and held eleven back.

**"What about physician privacy?"**
Everything shown is public professional record — published work,
registered trials, federal payment disclosures. There's a
report-data-issue and an opt-out request path on every profile.
*(Note: the opt-out mechanism is specced but not yet enforced —
don't overclaim if pressed.)*

**"Can it do other therapeutic areas?"**
NSCLC is fully built. Atopic dermatitis is the second, partially
built. The architecture is TA-scoped throughout — the constraint
is data ingestion time, not engineering.

**"How current is it?"**
Publications weekly. Trial status weekly. Trial discovery and
Medicare are less frequent, and the surfaces state their own
as-of dates. Nothing claims to be live that isn't.

## Two things not to demo

**Follow-ups** — still on the old design system and visually
inconsistent with everything else.

**The Public Conversation** — the social corpus is 96 percent one
conference, and if someone drills into it the framing gets hard
to defend on the fly.
