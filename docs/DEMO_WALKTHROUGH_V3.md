# FieldMark — demo walkthrough, v3

4 August 2026. Replaces the version pulled after the Hussein
data-integrity issue.

Running time: ~18 minutes at a comfortable pace. Cut points marked.

---

## What changed from v2, and why

Four things.

**The Hussein beat is gone.** Replaced with a different community
physician. Verify the figures yourself before recording — that is
what cost you the last video.

**Telescope is now SkyView**, and the navigation has been rebuilt
entirely. Nine items: PEOPLE · DRUGS · TRIALS · CONGRESSES ·
INSTITUTIONS · INTELLIGENCE · SOCIAL · PULSE · SKYVIEW, with
search on its own row. There is no HOME item — the wordmark is
home. There is no TERRITORY item.

**Four surfaces are new since v2** — Trials, The Week,
Institutions, and a rebuilt Pulse. Three of them are in this
script.

**And there is a second differentiator now**, alongside the
system-of-memory argument: the platform states what it cannot
see. After the last incident, that is not a defensive posture —
it is the strongest thing you can lead with.

---

## Before you record — five checks

1. **Walk the whole path once, signed in.** Several surfaces were
   rebuilt in the last 72 hours.
2. **Verify every number you plan to say aloud.** The old script's
   figures are stale in several places, flagged below.
3. **Warm the brief** for whichever HCP you use in Beat 11.
4. **Rehearse the Intelligence question.** The content check
   rejects recommendation phrasing, off-label vocabulary and any
   real physician name. It will bounce you live if you improvise.
5. **Pick a community physician and check their figures against
   source.** Do not reuse Hussein.

## BEAT 1 — The problem (75 sec)

**Nav:** blank slide or desktop. Browser not visible.

> Before I show you anything, I want to name the problem we set
> out to solve.

> Today's MSL tools are excellent at telling you who an
> investigator is. Their publications, their credentials, their
> network — what they've done.

> They're much weaker at helping you remember what you've learned
> from them. Over months, over years, across dozens of scientific
> conversations.

> The data systems in Medical Affairs are mature. The systems for
> building institutional memory from scientific engagement aren't.

> That's the problem FieldMark is built around. Everything I'm
> about to show you is evidence for whether we've solved it.

*[Pause two seconds]*

> One framing note. What you'll see is FieldMark for NSCLC — our
> launch indication. The architecture is therapeutic-area
> agnostic; atopic dermatitis is our second and it's partially
> built. NSCLC is just where the substrate is deepest.

## BEAT 2 — Cohorts, and one commitment (45 sec)

**Nav:** still no clicking.

> FieldMark organises investigators into three cohorts, because
> the questions you ask about each are fundamentally different.

> **Established** investigators are the ones who matter today —
> senior authors, trial chairs, guideline writers.

> **Rising Stars** are the investigators who'll matter tomorrow,
> and they're the hardest to identify with traditional tools,
> because they don't yet have the citation counts or the chair
> positions.

> **Community** physicians are where the medicine actually
> reaches patients, and they require a different intelligence
> model entirely.

> Three cohorts, three models. We'll look at all three.

*[Pause]*

> And one commitment before we start, because it runs through
> everything. **Every number on this platform traces to a source,
> and where we don't know something, we say so rather than
> showing you a zero.**

> You'll see that repeatedly. It's deliberate. An MSL taking a
> figure into a physician conversation needs to know which
> numbers are load-bearing and which aren't.

## BEAT 4 — Established: the profile (90 sec)

**Nav:** click a top-ranked established HCP from the portfolio
chips — Heymach or equivalent.

⚠️ **Verify the rank and score before recording.** The
established cohort was refiltered on 2 August — 271 industry and
regulatory employees removed — so every rank below 168 shifted.
The old script's "99, 100, 95 — ranked 8th US, 51st globally" is
almost certainly wrong now.

> Let's start with the Established cohort. This is [name],
> [institution] — one of the global leaders in NSCLC research.

> Before any field interaction, FieldMark has already assembled
> his scientific profile: publication history, collaborator
> network, an explainable scoring model.

**Scroll to the score band.**

> The Established score decomposes into two ranking signals and
> one context signal.

> **Scientific Influence** — publication leadership, citation
> impact, guideline authorship.
> **Network Influence** — collaboration network centrality across
> a decade of NSCLC co-authorship.
> **Pharma Engagement** — Open Payments breadth, shown as context
> and explicitly excluded from the ranking.

**This is worth a sentence, because it changed and it matters:**

> Pharma used to be in the composite. We took it out, because
> only about a third of the cohort has Open Payments data
> attributable to NSCLC — so weighting it was measuring whether
> we'd matched someone to a federal registry, not how engaged
> they are. Rather than let a coverage gap masquerade as a
> signal, we show it and don't score on it.

> The platform isn't asking the MSL to teach it who this person
> is. It already knows. The question is what it does with that.

## BEAT 6 — The different question (2 min — SLOW)

**Nav:** scroll to the Belief Profile.

This is the strongest intellectual beat in the demo. Do not rush.

> Most platforms in this space — H1, Veeva, the systems you know
> — are excellent at one thing: telling you who an investigator
> is. That's information retrieval.

*[Pause two seconds]*

> We deliberately answered a different question.

*[Pause two seconds]*

> Not "what has this investigator published?" — every system can
> answer that.

**Scroll slowly through the positions.**

> Each one synthesised from their publication corpus. Each one
> linked to the specific papers that support it. Banded by how
> strongly they're asserted — what they strongly advocate, what

> This isn't a topic list. It's a coherent scientific worldview,
> extracted from a decade of senior-author publications and
> structured into positions an MSL can actually work with.

*[Pause]*

> And everything here is explainable. Every position links to its

> blind trust.

**One honest note, if the evidence rail is capped:**

> The evidence shown per position is a sample — the full list is
> one click away. We'd rather show you four sourced papers and

## BEAT 8 — Rising Star scoring (75 sec)

**Nav:** scroll up to the Rising Star score block. ⚠️ Verify all
four sub-scores and the collaborator growth figure.

> While we're here, the Rising Star scoring is worth pausing on.

> This cohort is arguably the most strategically important for
> Medical Affairs, and the hardest to identify with traditional
> tools. These are the investigators who matter tomorrow, before
> they're chairing steering committees.

> The score decomposes into four signals.

> **Scientific Momentum** — how fast publication output is
> accelerating, recent five years against the earlier five.
> **Network Momentum** — how quickly the co-authorship network is
> expanding.
> **Scientific Visibility** — the current published footprint as a
> senior author.
> **Network Visibility** — current centrality in the co-authorship
> graph. Not just how many collaborators, but how connected they
> are to each other. Investigators who bridge clusters score
> higher than ones embedded in a single tight group.

> Momentum asks how fast someone is becoming a louder voice.
> Visibility asks where they stand today. Those are different
> questions and most tools conflate them.

## BEAT 12 — Field to Strategy, and the peer layer (90 sec)

**Nav:** `/me/insights`.

> Everything so far is the individual MSL view. The
> organisational value is in how field intelligence connects back
> to the rest of Medical Affairs.

> This is the Field Insights surface. Every observation captured,
> organised by HCP, by category, by date. Each tied to the
> published belief it reinforces or challenges.

**Hover the Share button.**

> And it's shareable. The MSL doesn't translate field
> observations into a separate weekly report. They share the
> structured view directly with Medical Strategy.

> In most pharma organisations, strategy flows down as messaging
> and field flows up as anecdotes. This turns field observation
> into a structured artefact Strategy can act on.

**Navigate to a profile's Field Intelligence / peer section.**

> There's also a peer layer. When multiple MSLs track the same
> investigator, the platform aggregates what the field
> collectively knows. Others can confirm an insight, partially
> confirm it, or dispute it from their own engagements.

> A single MSL's intelligence on an HCP is valuable. The field's
> collective intelligence, structured and validated by people who
> actually engage with the investigator, is something different.

## BEAT 13 — Intelligence, live (2 min)

**Nav:** INTELLIGENCE, then a bibliography, then create a thread.

Do this live. It's the most participatory moment in the demo.

> Anchored discussion. Every thread is anchored to a published
> paper — a thread can't exist without one, and the anchor defines
> what's on topic. Topic discussion only: no HCP names in posts,
> no product claims, no discussion of unapproved use.

**Point at the disclosure banner.**

> Note the labelling. Content marked SEEDED is fabricated for
> compliance review. Content marked LIVE is authored here by a
> verified MSL — and LIVE posts are checked for recommendation
> language, off-label content and physician names before
> publishing.

**Go to a bibliography, pick a paper, create the thread.**

> So here's the workflow. I'm on a physician's publication list. I
> find a paper I genuinely want to discuss. I click through, and I
> ask a question anchored to it.

**Type the rehearsed question. Submit. Land on the thread.**

> And there it is, marked LIVE, alongside the seeded content and
> clearly distinguished from it.

> That's the crowdsourced layer — it starts from the literature,
> not from a blank box. Which is what makes it compliant, and
> what makes it useful.

> This is the cohort nobody else covers. Every KOL database has

## BEAT 15 — The close (60 sec)

**Nav:** back to Home, or a blank slide.

> That's the tour. The features matter, but they're not the
> story.

> **The story is that this doesn't quite fit the existing
> category.** H1, Veeva, the platforms you know are systems of
> information retrieval. Who is this investigator? What have they
> published?

> FieldMark is a system of scientific memory and reasoning. What
> has this investigator consistently argued? What have we learned
> from engaging with them? How should that change our next
> conversation?

*[Pause]*

> Three things I'd want you to take away.

> **One.** The rising-star and community cohorts are the point.
> Everyone has the top fifty names. Nobody has the early-career
> investigator whose trajectory says they'll matter in eighteen
> months, or the community physician treating your patients today.

> **Two.** The honesty is the product, not an apology for it.
> Every surface tells you what it can't see. That's what makes
> the rest of it usable in front of a physician.

> **Three.** It refreshes. This isn't a database you license once
> a year. Publications ingest weekly, trial status refreshes
> weekly, the cohorts rescore, and the movement accrues. A live
> intelligence subscription, not a directory.

*[Pause]*

> If this works, every interaction makes the platform smarter —
> not just for the individual MSL, but eventually for the whole
> medical affairs organisation. The goal isn't just to prepare
> for the next conversation. It's to preserve the institutional
> scientific memory that usually disappears when the meeting
> ends.

> Thanks for taking the time.

## Questions you should expect

**"Where does the data come from?"**
PubMed and OpenAlex for publications and co-authorship.
ClinicalTrials.gov for trials. NPPES for identity and practice
location. CMS Open Payments and Medicare Part B for engagement and
administered volume. Public registries, all of them.

**"How do you know the physician is who you say?"**
We match by name against the federal registry, and we're explicit
that it's a match. Below a confidence threshold we hold it out of
the surface rather than showing it. On the social side we
hand-reviewed all one hundred and fifty-one attributions and held
eleven back.

**"What about physician privacy?"**
Everything shown is public professional record — published work,
registered trials, federal payment disclosures. There's a
report-data-issue path and an opt-out request on every profile.
*(The opt-out mechanism is specced but not yet enforced. Don't
overclaim if pressed.)*

**"Can it do other therapeutic areas?"**
NSCLC is fully built, atopic dermatitis is second and partially
built. The architecture is TA-scoped throughout — the constraint
is data ingestion time, not engineering.

**"How current is it?"**
Publications weekly. Trial status weekly. Trial discovery and
Medicare are less frequent, and every surface states its own
as-of date. Nothing claims to be live that isn't.

**"What happened with the last version of this demo?"**
If it comes up, answer it straight. You found a data integrity
problem in a figure you'd shown, pulled the video, traced the
error, fixed it, and rebuilt the surface so the same class of
error would be visible rather than silent. That is a better
answer than most vendors could give.
