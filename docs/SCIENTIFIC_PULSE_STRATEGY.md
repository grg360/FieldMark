# Scientific Pulse — Strategy & Advisor Reframe (2026-07-22)

## Origin: the Commons advisor review
The "Commons" (cross-company MSL discussion forum) was reviewed by an outside advisor. Verdict:
**do NOT launch a cross-company discussion forum at the start.** Not because it's illegal by definition,
but because the legal/compliance/trust risks far exceed the product risk currently being solved.

### The real risks (advisor reframe — NOT primarily antitrust)
We had over-indexed on antitrust. The advisor identified three risks that kill the forum BEFORE antitrust matters:
1. **Corporate policy / compliance uncertainty.** MSLs won't post if unsure Legal/Compliance approves. Uncertainty
   alone kills adoption. A perfectly legal forum can die because every MSL's rational move is "don't risk it."
2. **Competitive intelligence leakage.** Even purely scientific posts ("IL-31 questions increased this week")
   leak field dynamics (customer behavior, what MSLs are hearing). CRITICAL: AI cannot reliably separate
   "acceptable scientific observation" from "competitively sensitive field insight" — the categories OVERLAP.
   This directly defeats the proposed AI-moderation-gate firewall: it assumes a clean classification boundary
   that does not exist.
3. **Trust / self-censorship.** MSLs knowing competitors are watching won't post their best observations →
   the forum is thin even if safe and adopted.

### Why the firewall ("discuss the science, not the person/strategy") is necessary but NOT sufficient
Meaning isn't determined solely by post text. "Interesting increase in IL-31 questions this week" is scientific
AND reveals field intelligence simultaneously. No classifier reliably separates these.

## The pivot: it's not DISCUSSION, it's CONSENSUS
Instead of MSLs talking to each other (user-generated content + moderation + antitrust + corporate policy +
privacy = "entirely new businesses"), surface **aggregate intelligence without exposing conversations:**

> "Across 127 verified MSL organizations: interest in IL-31 increased this month. Pediatric sequencing
> questions doubled. Barrier dysfunction remains the dominant scientific topic."

- No companies identified. No posts visible. Only aggregate trends.
- Captures ~the same moat (community-derived intelligence incumbents can't replicate) WITHOUT the UGC/
  moderation/antitrust/corporate-policy tail.
- The moat imagined from the Commons "may emerge in a different form" — the system that synthesizes what the
  field is thinking WITHOUT requiring users to expose themselves publicly.

## Recommended build order (advisor)
Postpone the Commons. Double down on making Field Intelligence indispensable via:
- **Scientific Pulse** — AI-generated scientific change detection from publications, conferences, guidelines,
  trials, and (later) aggregate cross-org signal.
- **Organizational memory + team collaboration WITHIN each customer org** (internal discussion = zero competitor
  issue, high value). This is the safe version of "MSLs posting."
- Personalized watchlists + alerts.
- Territory intelligence.
- Consensus synthesis from public sources (v1 needs no cross-org data at all — public sources alone tell a
  compelling "what the field is thinking" story).

Revisit a carefully-designed, lawyered cross-company capability YEARS later, only if customers ask to opt in,
and only once FieldMark is "the place Medical Affairs teams already live" (credibility + legal resources to do
it right).

## Why Pulse is the better *build* (not just safer)
- It's AI + data synthesis (core strength), not moderation/compliance infrastructure (a slog).
- Plugs into what already exists: research themes (hcp_research_themes_v2), social/conference capture
  (twitter_capture / social_update), the weekly diff. Largely aggregating/surfacing already-computed signals.
- No legal review gating the build — can ship it.
- v1 = public sources only (no anonymity threshold needed). Cross-org aggregate is a later layer that only
  needs enough orgs to anonymize.

## Design question for the build session
What signals do we already compute that, aggregated (across public sources for v1, across orgs later), tell a
compelling "here's what the field is thinking / what changed this week" story? (Theme momentum, emerging
mechanisms, conference signal spikes, rising-star movement, trial/publication velocity shifts.)
